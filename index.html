INDEPENDENT STUDY / REVISION V

The exterior is real geometry.
The heart is a concept.

The exterior is based on “Rolex Datejust” by Yevhen Artamonov. The model has 37 named objects, a closed caseback and a dial that says Day-Date. We preserve the artist’s model without claiming it is an accurate or authenticated Rolex reference.

Hands, weekday and date follow the viewer’s device-local clock. Calendar textures replace the original fixed lettering; no static date is left underneath. The source has no internal movement. The exploded presentation and Chapter 03 use a separately built manual-wind / auxiliary chronograph mechanism. Chapter 03 includes focus views, layer spacing, bridge visibility, pause and playback speed controls. It is an illustrative motion study, not a model of a Datejust or Day-Date calibre. Its stopwatch controls do not imply that the supplied watch is a chronograph.

How to explore

Scroll directly scrubs the scene: slow, fast, stop or reverse at your own pace. Precise mode softens large mouse-wheel impulses as they arrive, without storing them. The bottom Scroll button switches to unmodified native wheel input. Touch uses normal browser scrolling. There is no queued catch-up. Index buttons make a short transition that wheel or touch input cancels. The bottom scrub bar jumps directly to any point. Play follows the whole sequence. Select opens part details without repainting steel; Isolate dims the rest; Deep lets you drag to orbit and use the wheel to zoom. Escape returns to the scene.

Bracelet objects in the supplied file contain several links. They separate as rigid sections; there is no hidden per-link rig. Exterior separation and curved reassembly are presentation animations, not instructions for dismantling a watch. Colour treatments are artistic, not official variants.
